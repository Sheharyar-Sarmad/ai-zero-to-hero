
<!-- Measure of central spread -->
Measures of central spread are statistical measures that show how much the values in a dataset are spread out or scattered around the central value (mean, median, or mode). They tell us how far the data values are from each other and from the center of the dataset.

<!-- Range -->
Range is the difference between the largest value and the smallest value in a dataset. Range tells us how far apart the highest and lowest values are in a dataset.

<!-- Formula -->
Range = Maximum Value − Minimum Value

<!-- Example -->
Example

Data:
12, 18, 25, 30, 40

Maximum value = 40
Minimum value = 12

So, Range = 40 − 12 = 28

<!-- Interquartile Range(IQR) -->
Interquartile Range (IQR) is the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and the first quartile (Q1). It measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data. IQR tells us how spread out the middle half (50%) of the data is.

<!-- Formula -->
IQR = Q3−Q1

<!-- Example -->
Data (already arranged):
2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18

Step 1: Find Q1
Lower half: 2, 4, 6, 8
Q1 = 24 + 6 = 5

Step 2: Find Q3
Upper half: 12, 14, 16, 18
Q3=214+16=15

Step 3: Find IQR
IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 15 − 5 = 10

Answer: IQR = 10

<!-- Conclusion -->

If the IQR is low, the data is close to the median. If the IQR is high, the data is highly spread from the center.

In one sentence :
Low IQR → Middle 50% of the data is close together (less spread).
High IQR → Middle 50% of the data is far apart (more spread).
