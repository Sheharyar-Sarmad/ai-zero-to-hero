# Descriptive Statistics vs Inferential Statistics

## Statistics

Statistics is the branch of mathematics that deals with collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting data.

Statistics is divided into two main branches:

1. Descriptive Statistics
2. Inferential Statistics

---

# 1. Descriptive Statistics

## Definition

Descriptive Statistics is the branch of statistics that summarizes, organizes, and presents data in a meaningful way.

It describes the characteristics of a dataset but does not make predictions or conclusions about a larger population.

### Purpose

- Organize data
- Summarize data
- Present data clearly
- Describe what happened

### Common Tools

#### Measures of Central Tendency
- Mean
- Median
- Mode

#### Measures of Dispersion
- Range
- Variance
- Standard Deviation
- Interquartile Range (IQR)

#### Graphs and Charts
- Histogram
- Bar Chart
- Pie Chart
- Frequency Polygon
- Ogive
- Box Plot

### Example

Suppose a class has the following marks:

```text
80, 85, 90, 75, 70
```

Descriptive Statistics can calculate:

- Mean = 80
- Median = 80
- Highest score = 90
- Lowest score = 70

These values simply describe the dataset.

---

# 2. Inferential Statistics

## Definition

Inferential Statistics is the branch of statistics that uses sample data to make conclusions, predictions, or decisions about an entire population.

### Purpose

- Make predictions
- Test hypotheses
- Estimate population parameters
- Draw conclusions

### Common Tools

#### Estimation
- Point Estimation
- Confidence Intervals

#### Hypothesis Testing
- Z-Test
- T-Test
- Chi-Square Test
- ANOVA

#### Probability Concepts
- Sampling Distribution
- Central Limit Theorem
- Bayes' Theorem

#### Regression and Correlation
- Linear Regression
- Correlation Analysis

### Example

A researcher surveys 500 voters from a city of 1,000,000 voters.

If 60% of the sample supports a candidate, Inferential Statistics may conclude that approximately 60% of the entire population supports that candidate.

The conclusion is inferred from the sample.

---

# Key Difference

| Feature | Descriptive Statistics | Inferential Statistics |
|----------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Purpose | Describe data | Make predictions and conclusions |
| Data Used | Entire dataset or sample | Usually sample data |
| Output | Tables, graphs, averages | Predictions and decisions |
| Focus | What happened? | What is likely to happen? |
| Uncertainty | No | Yes |
| Uses Probability | Rarely | Extensively |

---

# Easy Analogy

Imagine a school with 1000 students.

## Descriptive Statistics

You calculate:

- Average age = 15 years
- Average marks = 78
- Highest marks = 98

You are describing existing data.

## Inferential Statistics

You survey only 100 students and predict:

> "The average marks of all 1000 students are likely around 78."

You are making an inference about the population using a sample.

---

# Population vs Sample

## Population

The entire group being studied.

Example:

```text
All students in a school
```

## Sample

A smaller subset selected from the population.

Example:

```text
100 students chosen from the school
```

Descriptive Statistics can describe either a sample or population.

Inferential Statistics uses a sample to draw conclusions about the population.

# One-Line Difference

Descriptive Statistics describes the data you have, while Inferential Statistics uses sample data to make conclusions about data you do not have.
