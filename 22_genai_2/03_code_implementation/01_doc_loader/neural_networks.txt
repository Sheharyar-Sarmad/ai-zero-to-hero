========================================================================
NEURAL NETWORK RESEARCH GUIDE: FROM FUNDAMENTALS TO FRONTIERS
A Personal Study Guide for a 16-Year-Old Self-Taught AI Engineer
========================================================================

PART 1: THE CORE FOUNDATIONS (How a Neural Network Thinks)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The Perceptron (The Building Block)
   - The simplest neural network. Takes inputs, multiplies them by "weights", 
     adds a "bias", and passes it through an "activation function".
   - Analogy: A light switch. If the sum of inputs is strong enough, it 
     turns on (fires).

2. Activation Functions (The Decision Makers)
   - ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit): The industry standard. If x > 0, return x. 
     Else return 0. (Solves the vanishing gradient problem).
   - Sigmoid/Softmax: Squashes numbers into probabilities (0 to 1). Used 
     for binary or multi-class classification (e.g., "Is this spam?" or 
     "What digit is this?").
   - GELU/SiLU: Modern activation functions used in Transformers (like GPT).

3. Forward Propagation vs. Backpropagation
   - Forward: Data goes in, prediction comes out.
   - Backward (Backprop): The network calculates the error (Loss) and uses 
     Calculus (Chain Rule) to go backward and update the weights. This is 
     literally how the model "learns". 

4. Loss Functions (The Scorecard)
   - MSE (Mean Squared Error): For regression (predicting a number).
   - Cross-Entropy: For classification (predicting a category).

PART 2: THE ARCHITECTURES (How They Evolved)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Feedforward Neural Networks (MLPs)
   - Basic layers of neurons. Good for tabular data, bad for images or sequences.

2. CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks)
   - Designed for images. Uses "filters" that slide over the image to detect 
     edges, shapes, and patterns. 
   - Key Papers: "ImageNet Classification with Deep CNNs" (AlexNet, 2012), 
     "Deep Residual Learning" (ResNet, 2015).

3. RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs (Sequential Data)
   - Designed for text, audio, and time-series. They have a "memory" of 
     previous inputs.
   - The Problem: Vanishing gradients. They forget the beginning of long 
     sequences.
   - The Fix: LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) and GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit). 
     They use "gates" to decide what to remember and what to forget.

4. Transformers (The Current King)
   - Introduced in 2017 ("Attention Is All You Need"). 
   - Uses "Self-Attention" to look at all parts of a sentence simultaneously, 
     rather than one by one. 
   - This is the architecture behind BERT, GPT, Llama, and Mistral.
   - Key Concepts: Multi-Head Attention, Positional Encoding, Encoder/Decoder.

PART 3: TRAINING & OPTIMIZATION (The Practical Side)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Overfitting vs. Underfitting
   - Overfitting: The model memorizes the training data but fails on new data.
   - Underfitting: The model is too simple and can't learn the patterns.
   - Fixes: More data, Dropout, Regularization (L1/L2), Early Stopping.

2. Optimizers (How we update weights)
   - SGD (Stochastic Gradient Descent): Simple, but slow.
   - Adam / AdamW: The modern standard. Adapts the learning rate for each 
     weight automatically. (Used to train almost all LLMs).

3. Data Leakage (The Silent Killer)
   - When information from the test set accidentally leaks into the training 
     set. The model looks smart, but it's cheating. You must always split 
     your data BEFORE any preprocessing.

4. Regularization
   - Dropout: Randomly turns off neurons during training to force the 
     network to be more robust.
   - Batch Normalization: Normalizes the inputs to each layer to stabilize 
     and speed up training.

PART 4: LANDMARK RESEARCH PAPERS (The Must-Reads)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017)
   - The paper that introduced the Transformer. The foundation of all modern AI.

2. "Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition" (He et al., 2015)
   - Introduced ResNet. Allowed networks to be hundreds of layers deep 
     without losing performance.

3. "Generative Adversarial Networks" (Goodfellow et al., 2014)
   - Two networks fighting each other (Generator vs. Discriminator) to 
     create realistic fake data (images, audio).

4. "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models" (Ho et al., 2020)
   - The foundation of Stable Diffusion and Midjourney. Learns to reverse 
     a noise process to generate images from scratch.

5. "BERT" (Devlin et al., 2018) & "GPT-3" (Brown et al., 2020)
   - The papers that popularized Large Language Models and transfer learning.

PART 5: MODERN RESEARCH FRONTIERS (Where the field is going)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
   - Connecting LLMs to external databases (like ChromaDB) so they don't 
     hallucinate and can access private data. (You are building this now!).

2. Agentic Workflows
   - Giving LLMs tools (web search, code execution, API calls) so they can 
     act autonomously to solve complex tasks.

3. Mechanistic Interpretability
   - "Opening the black box." Researchers are trying to reverse-engineer 
     exactly what specific neurons and circuits in an LLM are doing.

4. Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA / QLoRA)
   - Instead of retraining the whole model, you freeze most of it and train 
     a tiny set of "adapter" weights. This lets you fine-tune massive models 
     on a single consumer GPU.

5. Mixture of Experts (MoE)
   - Instead of using the whole brain for every question, the model routes 
     the input to a specific "expert" sub-network. (Used in Mixtral and GPT-4).

PART 6: ADVICE FOR THE SELF-TAUGHT ENGINEER
------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Read on ArXiv (arxiv.org) and use "Papers with Code" to find implementations.
- Don't just read. Implement. Even if it's a tiny version.
- Use Hugging Face to explore pre-trained models and datasets.
- Use Google Colab for free GPUs (T4).
- Keep building end-to-end products. Theory is useless without application.
